# Veilfall — Progression to Level 30

**Status:** approved design direction
**Version:** 0.1
**Language:** Ukrainian-first player experience
**Purpose:** connect the existing vertical slices to the first long-term progression milestone

## 1. Product goal

Level 30 is not only a numerical threshold. It is the moment when an initiated
player becomes an economically relevant participant in the persistent world.

The first campaign teaches and unlocks systems in this order:

```text
survive and learn combat
→ see the living front
→ learn basic crafting
→ investigate both factions
→ choose a faction
→ contribute to the war
→ unlock clans, trade, and ritual crafting
→ defeat a Seal Bearer
→ enter the summon-seal economy
```

Content names in this document remain configurable.

## 2. Progression cadence

The journey must not be balanced as a one-session race.

- levels 1–10: approximately 2–4 days of moderate play;
- levels 11–20: approximately 1–2 additional weeks;
- levels 21–30: approximately 2–4 additional weeks.

These are balance targets, not hard time gates. Campaign progression must come
from meaningful play, preparation, crafting, and combat rather than mandatory
idle waits.

The initial level cap is 99. Progress after level 99 shifts toward Awakening,
equipment Ascension, item development, builds, sets, and endgame activities
rather than immediately extending ordinary levels.

## 3. Sources of power

Veilfall separates the hero's capabilities from the hero's numerical power.

### 3.1 Hero level

Hero level unlocks locations, ordinary progression, base attributes, skill
access, and eligibility for systems.

### 3.2 Awakening

**Awakening** is the hero's advanced knowledge system. It must not be called
Ascension.

Awakening opens a large knowledge tree containing:

- combat techniques that interact with equipment mechanics;
- crafting research and improved experiment feedback;
- recipe-discovery skills;
- exploration, rare-NPC, and hidden-route abilities;
- survival, gathering, and limited daily-yield abilities;
- specializations such as warrior, researcher, alchemist, explorer, labyrinth
  specialist, and front commander.

Awakening adds choices and new ways to use power. It must not make equipment
irrelevant by supplying most raw damage, armor, or health.

Example: `Greed` improves the first several meaningful daily rewards or allows
a small bound resource choice. It must not create unlimited tradeable premium
resources passively.

### 3.3 Equipment

Equipment supplies the majority of numerical combat power. Valuable equipment
must remain meaningful throughout the game.

Each item has, independently:

- item level from 1 to 99;
- rarity;
- roll quality within an allowed range;
- equipment slot and definition;
- set membership;
- upgrade state;
- rune or enchantment state;
- binding and ownership rules;
- origin and lineage;
- Ascension state.

At the same item level, the minimum primary roll of a higher rarity must exceed
the maximum primary roll of the preceding rarity. A level-1 item of one rarity
is not required to exceed a level-99 item of the preceding rarity: enforcing
that rule would make item level meaningless.

### 3.4 Equipment Ascension

**Ascension** is reserved for equipment. It is a catastrophically expensive,
long-term transformation into an artifact of a different order.

An Ascended item must provide a clearly transformative reward:

- a very large increase to its primary combat values;
- transformed or substantially stronger existing properties;
- unique active or passive mechanics;
- a development branch for the artifact;
- a new name, presentation, history, and Chronicle event;
- a long useful life with later development rather than rapid replacement.

Ascension is not a routine `+10%` upgrade. Exact values are determined only
after combat balance exists, but months of discovery and material collection
must produce a reward that visibly changes what the character can do.

## 4. Campaign structure

### Levels 1–5 — The first rupture

The hero awakens near a damaged section of the Veil and reaches a temporary
camp with the help of a neutral survivor.

The campaign introduces:

- explicit combat commands;
- enemy intent;
- health and archetype resources;
- experience and attributes;
- inventory and equipment;
- an early moral choice involving wounded members of opposing factions.

The first weapon is granted by the story. After that, complete equipment drops
randomly rather than from every boss.

### Levels 6–10 — The Broken Watchpost

The player first sees the scripted living front. Territory ownership, patrols,
and Chronicle reports can change without simulating individual NPC battles.

The campaign introduces:

- basic resource drops;
- the Workshop and public Craft Codex;
- short recipes;
- a temporary NPC companion;
- target choice and group-combat fundamentals;
- the first campaign boss at level 10.

### Levels 11–15 — Two sides of the Veil

The player completes short stories for both factions before choosing either.
Neither faction is presented as wholly good or evil.

This stage introduces:

- the first 10 rooms of the Goblin Cave labyrinth;
- labyrinth energy;
- route choices and random events;
- the personal Research Journal;
- recipe clues and experimental crafting;
- daily objectives.

### Level 16 — The oath

The player chooses a faction only after learning both positions. The game may
recommend the weaker faction using recent active power rather than registered
account totals. Accepting the recommendation may grant a limited onboarding
benefit, never a permanent combat advantage.

Faction themes share the same UX and layout. Only palette, emblem, ornament,
icons, selected terminology, and atmospheric content differ.

A player may later change faction once through a serious narrative event,
provisionally called Renunciation of the Oath. It removes clan membership and
faction reputation, temporarily suspends war participation, affects faction
equipment, and remains recorded in the character history.

### Levels 16–20 — The real front

The player gains faction reputation and contributes to the persistent war by:

- defeating patrols and commanders;
- escorting supplies;
- scouting;
- sabotaging enemy positions;
- defending objectives;
- optionally accepting matched player encounters.

Scripted attacks create pressure but do not arbitrarily erase a player-led
capture. Captured territory receives a protection period. Level-banded fronts
and power-aware matching prevent strong players from farming beginners.

### Levels 21–25 — The price of knowledge

The campaign reveals the existence of summon seals and introduces:

- crafted resources unavailable as ordinary drops;
- multi-ingredient recipes;
- catalysts of different quality;
- parallel crafting stations or queues;
- the market;
- full clan membership and treasury history;
- a bound tutorial ritual that teaches summoning without entering the tradeable
  seal economy.

### Levels 26–29 — The trail

The player investigates a future Seal Bearer and must demonstrate readiness
across combat, crafting, the labyrinth, and the front. A level-29 herald acts as
a mechanical rehearsal but does not award a tradeable seal.

### Level 30 — The first Seal Bearer

Eligibility requires:

- level 30;
- completion of the first campaign;
- faction choice;
- access to the appropriate front;
- minimum combat readiness;
- completion of the introductory seal investigation.

The Seal Bearer is a rare solo miniboss with multiple mechanics, not an ordinary
patrol enemy. The first campaign encounter and later guaranteed weekly encounter
provide a limited retry window. Encounter opportunity is guaranteed for an
eligible active character; the seal itself is guaranteed only on victory.

The first victory unlocks the summon ladder, seal reforging, and the tradeable
seal economy.

## 5. Summon seals

Summon seals are permanent, transferable, tradeable, and consumable assets.
They may be:

- stored indefinitely;
- gifted or exchanged directly;
- listed on the official market;
- contributed to a clan treasury;
- required as a disclosed clan contribution;
- reforged into another boss branch;
- consumed to summon a boss.

They do not expire and have no storage tax. Consumption by summons and reforging
creates continuing demand.

### 5.1 Weekly acquisition

Eligible level-30 characters can earn one guaranteed Seal Bearer encounter
opportunity through meaningful weekly activity. Up to four additional encounters
may occur with diminishing probability after each appearance.

The system guarantees an encounter, not an automatic seal. A player must defeat
the miniboss. The miniboss scales by broad level band and gains mechanics at
higher bands, but it must not scale perfectly to current equipment: improving
gear must remain valuable.

Level, campaign, readiness, and weekly activity make additional-account farming
expensive. Behavioral anti-automation controls remain necessary because
progression friction alone cannot prevent organized farming.

### 5.2 Summon ladder

An initial seal begins a multi-step chain:

```text
Seal Bearer
→ initial seal
→ boss I
→ seal II and exclusive trophy
→ boss II
→ seal III and better trophy
→ boss III
→ seal IV
→ clan-grade or final boss
```

Each later boss is mechanically and numerically stronger. Later stages may
require a group or clan. Boss-exclusive trophies cannot be crafted from ordinary
materials and are required for the highest crafting and Ascension paths.

Reforging the initial seal selects branches such as cursed knight, fallen elf,
or dark priest. Branch choice changes mechanics, trophies, equipment families,
and later summons. All seal tiers remain transferable unless a future content
definition explicitly states a justified exception.

## 6. Resource model

Resource rarity and resource origin are independent dimensions.

### 6.1 Origins

1. **Gathered or dropped:** found through battles, expeditions, fronts, and
   labyrinths.
2. **Crafted:** created from known or discovered recipes and unavailable as
   ordinary level drops.
3. **Boss-exclusive:** granted only by specified summoned bosses or endgame
   events; not reproducible from ordinary resources.

### 6.2 Rarity

Resources may range from common through divine. Origin does not force rarity:
a gathered plant from a hidden endgame location may be legendary, while an
ordinary crafted component may be uncommon.

## 7. Crafting model

Recipes are variable ingredient collections rather than one fixed formula.

A recipe may require:

- one to seven or more distinct ingredients;
- different quantities of every ingredient;
- one or more catalysts;
- item instances, resources, liquids, fragments, or trophies;
- a specific station, location, world state, title, time, or ingredient order;
- seconds, hours, days, or an exceptional multi-week ritual duration.

`base item + property + catalyst` remains one useful template for equipment
modification, not the universal crafting rule.

Long rituals run in a dedicated parallel queue. A 300-hour endgame ritual does
not block ordinary crafting. Rare resources, clan help, controlled locations,
and related objectives may accelerate it within configured limits.

Hidden recipes must be discoverable through coherent clues and recorded
experiments, not blind enumeration of millions of combinations.

## 8. Economy

Gold supports ordinary services. `Veil Shards` is the provisional scarce
progression currency used across advanced crafting, reforging, research,
high-level markets, clan projects, rituals, and equipment Ascension.

Veil Shards are earned in limited amounts from meaningful activities rather
than every ordinary kill. Permanent sinks include advanced crafting, market
fees, reforging, item development, clan projects, ritual launches, and
Ascension.

The target state is that an active player can always make progress toward more
Shards but cannot fund every attractive goal simultaneously. Deficit comes from
meaningful choices, not arbitrary deletion or expiration.

Summon seals are a second, commodity-backed exchange asset. Their price is
player-driven because they provide access to specific valuable content and are
destroyed on use.

## 9. Implementation order

The existing vertical slices remain the engineering proof points. This campaign
specification extends them in small playable increments:

1. finish and polish the existing first journey;
2. consolidate the city hub, resources, talents, clans, and clan boss into a
   coherent level-gated progression;
3. add item-level and rarity foundations before broad content production;
4. add crafting definitions, queues, and recipe knowledge;
5. add the scripted front and faction choice;
6. add the first labyrinth;
7. add trade and clan-treasury support for transferable assets;
8. add Seal Bearers, summon seals, reforging, and the first boss branch;
9. add Awakening only after ordinary levels, equipment, and crafting make its
   role clear;
10. add equipment Ascension only when its cost and transformative reward can be
    supported by real endgame content.

## 10. Non-goals for the first campaign

Before level 30, the game may foreshadow but does not expose:

- equipment Ascension;
- hero Awakening;
- top-tier rune trees;
- final labyrinth floors;
- the weekly central-altar war;
- divine crafting;
- final summon-ladder bosses.
